# Where every asset came from

Written 10 September 2026. The register the mission asked for, and the answer is
unusually short — which is itself the finding worth recording.

---

## 1. The rule this project already had

Five documents state it independently and none of them hedge:

> **Zero binary assets. Everything procedural.**
> — `CAMPAIGN.md` ("Non-negotiable"), `DISTRIBUTION.md`, `BACKLOG.md`,
> `FIRE-HAZARD.md`, `WHAT-THIS-GAME-IS.md`

That rule is why a licence audit of this repository is a short document. There is
**no third-party model, texture, sound or animation in the product.** Nothing was
downloaded from Sketchfab, Poly Haven, ambientCG, BlenderKit, Fab, CGTrader or
anywhere else; nothing was adapted from a museum scan or a heritage
reconstruction. Every warrior, weapon, helm, ground, sky, texture and sound
effect is generated — either by `src/game/client/characters.ts` and its
neighbours at runtime, or by `tools/blender/*.py` into a GLB this repository
built and owns.

Two consequences worth being explicit about, because they are the reasons a
register exists at all:

* **Commercial release is unencumbered by anything in `art/` or `src/`.** There
  is no attribution to carry, no non-commercial clause to trip over, no
  provenance that cannot be established.
* **Any future third-party asset is a new category, not an addition to an
  existing one.** §4 is the rule for that day.

## 3. The one thing the rule does not cover: two typefaces

**`src/app/layout.tsx:6` — `import { Cinzel, Alegreya } from "next/font/google"`.**

These are the only creative works in the shipped product that this project did
not make. "Zero binary assets" is true of the *game* and was never written with
type in mind, and `next/font/google` hides the fact well: it fetches at BUILD
time and self-hosts, so nothing is downloaded by a player and nothing is
committed to the repository. The result is real all the same —
**18 `.woff2` files land in `.next/static/media` on every build** and ship to
every player.

| Face | Used for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Cinzel** | display — headings, the game's name, HUD banners | `--font-display`, named in `globals.css` in five places |
| **Alegreya** | body — everything else | `--font-body` |

**Licensing: VERIFIED at the source, 10 Sep 2026**, against Google's own
`METADATA.pb` and `OFL.txt` in `github.com/google/fonts` — not from a specimen
page and not from memory.

| | Cinzel | Alegreya |
|---|---|---|
| Licence | **SIL Open Font License 1.1** | **SIL Open Font License 1.1** |
| Copyright | Copyright 2020 The Cinzel Project Authors | Copyright 2011 The Alegreya Project Authors |
| Designer | Natanael Gama | Juan Pablo del Peral, HT Fonts |
| Upstream | `NDISCOVER/Cinzel` | `huertatipografica/Alegreya` |
| Reserved Font Name | **none declared** | **none declared** |

OFL 1.1 permits commercial use, embedding, and modification. Its one real
restriction does not touch this project: the font may not be sold on its own,
only bundled — which is what shipping a game does.

**And the Reserved Font Name clause does not apply.** It is the obligation
easiest to trip over — modify a face, keep its name, and you are in breach — but
neither copyright line carries "with Reserved Font Name", so there is nothing
reserved to collide with even if the faces were ever subset or re-hinted.

The licence text is vendored at `public/licenses/Cinzel-OFL.txt` and
`Alegreya-OFL.txt`. OFL asks that the licence travel with the font, and
`next/font` copies the binaries into `.next/static/media` without them, so this
is the copy that satisfies it. They sit in `public/` rather than `docs/`
deliberately: that way they are SERVED alongside the fonts they cover rather
than living only in the repository.

**There is no open licensing question in this project.**

## 4. The rule for anything third-party, if that day comes

Nothing here needs it today. It is written down so the first person tempted has
a standard rather than a judgement call.

**Before anything is added, record in this file:** name · creator · source URL ·
licence and version · commercial use · modification · redistribution ·
attribution required · source poly count · rigged or not · textures · and what
it is actually for.

**Then classify it:**

* **USE** — CC0 or a licence plainly compatible with commercial release. Ships.
* **ADAPT** — usable, but as an input: a retopology source, a bake source, a
  proportion reference. The shipped mesh is still this project's.
* **REFERENCE ONLY** — informs the work, never enters the build. An
  archaeological reconstruction that cannot be licensed is still worth looking at.
* **REJECT** — non-commercial clauses, editorial-only, unclear rights, ripped
  commercial game assets, dubious re-uploads, or any provenance that cannot be
  established. Rejected is the default and the burden is on the asset.

**And two things this project's own history argues for keeping:**

1. **Never ship a raw high-poly source.** `gltftest` already reports the heaviest
   single file in the build every run — a head of long hair at 3.5 MB — and
   `public/authored` is 43 MB with no streaming policy. A downloaded sculpt is a
   reference and a bake source, not a shipped mesh.
2. **Verify licensing at the source, not from a listing page.** A marketplace
   card and the file's actual `LICENSE` disagree often enough that only one of
   them counts.
